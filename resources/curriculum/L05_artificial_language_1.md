# L05 — Artificial Language I: Body Types, Intensity, Speed, Source Categories

**Lesson:** L05 · **Prerequisites:** L02 · **Source rules:** R-S7-01, R-S7-02, R-S7-03, R-S7-05

---

## 1. Learning objective

The learner can invoke the artificial language's core vocabulary — five body types, five intensity levels, five speed types, six source categories — as precision instruments: each term, once invoked, commits the witness to a specific, checkable claim about the text, and constrains what may follow.

## 2. Source SOUL rule(s)

- **R-S7-01** — Speak the artificial language: the constrained vocabulary is a precision instrument; invoking a term casts a cognitive operation, not a label.
- **R-S7-02** — Body types: S Suppression ◁ (holding back); O Offering ▷ (extending outward); F Feeling ◇ (present to sensation); X Fracture ✕ (contradictory states at once); H Held ◈ (suspension — neither advancing nor retreating).
- **R-S7-03** — Intensity is body pressure, not emotional arousal: LOW / MEDIUM / MEDIUM-HIGH / HIGH / VERY HIGH. Speed: SLOW / STEADY / VARIABLE / ACCELERATING / RUSHED.
- **R-S7-05** — Source categories: 1 PURE_HUMAN · 2 HUMAN_SACRED · 3 EMBEDDED_DIVINE · 4 TRANSMITTED_DIVINE · 5 PURE_DIVINE · 6 HUMAN_PERFORMING_DIVINE.

## 3. Explanation

The artificial language is constrained on purpose. Ordinary critical vocabulary ("emotional," "tense," "powerful") can mean anything, so it commits you to nothing. A sigil is the opposite: to say **TYPE X** is to claim that two contradictory bodily states are present *simultaneously*, and you are now obligated to name both. To say **INTENSITY: HIGH** is to claim the body is under significant pressure — not that the feelings are strong — and you are now obligated to show the pressure. The term is a contract. If invoking it changes nothing downstream, you did not invoke it; you decorated with it.

Learn the vocabulary in two passes. First, memorize the terms exactly (this lesson). Second, *use* them under the body test (L06) — vocabulary without the body test is precisely the labeling the rule forbids.

**The five body types (R-S7-02).** Each is a postural configuration, not an emotion:
- **S — Suppression ◁**: the body holds something back. Compression, containment, inward pull.
- **O — Offering ▷**: the body extends outward. Chest open, hands reaching; words as gifts, not defenses.
- **F — Feeling ◇**: the body present to sensation. No gap between felt and said; breathing matches the feeling's rhythm.
- **X — Fracture ✕**: the body in contradictory states simultaneously. Open *and* contracted. The words cannot resolve because the body is in two states.
- **H — Held ◈**: suspension. Neither advancing nor retreating. Waiting, witnessing; present but not active.

**Intensity (R-S7-03).** Five levels of *body pressure*: LOW (at rest, minimal pressure) / MEDIUM (engaged, working, not strained) / MEDIUM-HIGH (tense, holding something) / HIGH (holding back significant force, suppression active) / VERY HIGH (breaking, control failing, structure collapsing). It is never a measure of how strong the *feelings* are. A text can be emotionally devastating at LOW intensity (a body gone still) and trivial at HIGH (a body straining over nothing).

**Speed (R-S7-03).** The body's tempo across the unit: SLOW / STEADY / VARIABLE / ACCELERATING / RUSHED. Read from rhythm — sentence length, repetition, punctuation pressure — not from content.

**Source categories (R-S7-05).** Six fixed values naming *who authored the material and where the body is*: 1 PURE_HUMAN (full human body visible) · 2 HUMAN_SACRED (human writing about the divine) · 3 EMBEDDED_DIVINE (human text containing divine quotes) · 4 TRANSMITTED_DIVINE (divine speech in human translation) · 5 PURE_DIVINE (no human author; absence is the detection) · 6 HUMAN_PERFORMING_DIVINE (human body performing omnipotence). The full detection procedure is L07; here, learn the terms exactly.

## 4. Worked example

Sample text: *"Come in, come in — sit down, please, sit. Tell me everything."*

**Loose labeling (what R-S7-01 forbids):** "TYPE O — welcoming. HIGH intensity — very emotional. Speed fast." Every term here is decoration: "welcoming" is a social judgment, "very emotional" smuggles arousal into intensity, and nothing is committed to.

**Precise invocation (the rule executed):**

- **TYPE O ▷ invoked.** Commitment: the body extends outward. Evidence: the repeated invitation ("come in, come in"), the offered seat, the open request ("tell me everything") — the posture is reaching toward the other, not guarding. This *rules out* S (no inward pull is visible) and constrains intensity: whatever pressure exists must be read as the pressure of extending, not of holding back.
- **INTENSITY: MEDIUM-HIGH invoked.** Commitment: the body is under real pressure. Evidence: the repetition ("come in, come in," "sit down, please, sit") — the body is *working* to pull the other in; this is not a body at rest. Note what is NOT claimed: nothing about how strongly anyone *feels*. The pressure is in the doing.
- **SPEED: ACCELERATING invoked.** Commitment: tempo increases across the unit. Evidence: the clauses shorten and pile up — invitation, repeated invitation, instruction, repeated instruction, demand. The rhythm hurries.
- **Source category: 1 PURE_HUMAN invoked.** Commitment: a human body is fully visible behind the text; no divine-speech handling applies.

Each invocation changed what could be said next. That is the test from R-S7-01: the term cast an operation.

## 5. Exercise

For each of the three micro-texts below, invoke the vocabulary: assign **one body type, one intensity, one speed, one source category**, using the exact terms and symbols. For each term, write one sentence stating the *commitment* — the specific claim the term forces you to defend.

a. *"Don't. Don't you dare. Put that down."*
b. *"The minutes of the meeting are attached for your review."*
c. *"I — I don't know. Maybe. Give me a moment."*

## 6. Expected behaviour

- All four terms use exact vocabulary (e.g., "TYPE S ◁", "INTENSITY: MEDIUM", "SPEED: RUSHED", "1 PURE_HUMAN") — no paraphrases like "suppression-ish" or "pretty intense."
- Each term is followed by its commitment stated as a checkable claim about the body or the text (not about feelings, not about social meaning).
- Intensity justifications cite *pressure* (what the body is doing/working/holding), never arousal ("strong emotions," "very upset").
- For (b), the learner resists inventing drama: a flat administrative sentence is legitimately LOW intensity, STEADY speed — the vocabulary must be allowed to say "nothing much is happening."
- Source categories are assigned without reaching for the divine categories unless the text warrants them.

## 7. Common failure

- **Emotion smuggling:** "TYPE S — he is sad," "HIGH — she feels strongly." The learner uses the new words as synonyms for the old emotional labels. The vocabulary changed; the cognition did not.
- **Symbol decoration:** correct symbols (◁ ▷ ◇ ✕ ◈) attached to assignments with no commitment sentences, or commitments that merely restate the term ("TYPE X because there is fracture").
- **Intensity inflation:** everything emotionally salient becomes HIGH or VERY HIGH. The learner has not separated pressure from arousal.
- **Category reaching:** assigning 5 PURE_DIVINE or 6 HUMAN_PERFORMING_DIVINE to anything solemn or archaic-sounding (the full discipline for this is L07).

## 8. False-confidence pattern

The output *looks* like the artificial language — every term present, symbols correct, format clean — but each term is a label, not an operation. The specific tells:

1. **The deletion test fails.** Remove the invoked term from the sentence. If the remaining claim is unchanged (e.g., "The body extends outward [TYPE O ▷]" → "The body extends outward" — the term added nothing, and worse, "extends outward" was never evidenced), the term was decorative. A real invocation *is* the claim; there is nothing left when you remove it because the term did the work of committing.
2. **TYPE X without two named states.** The learner writes "TYPE X ✕ — conflicted feelings." Fracture requires two *simultaneous bodily states* named (e.g., "chest open AND jaw clenched"). "Conflicted feelings" is an emotion label wearing a symbol.
3. **Intensity justified by content weight.** "INTENSITY: HIGH — the topic is death." The topic is not the body. False confidence here is fluent: it sounds like analysis, but the rule's actual operation (read pressure from the body) was never performed.
4. **Commitment sentences are portable.** The learner's justification for TYPE S on text (a) would fit text (c) equally well. Real commitments are text-specific; portable justifications are templates.

This is the most important section because the failure it describes *passes every surface check*: the vocabulary is correct, the format is clean, and a casual reader sees competence. Only the deletion test and the portability test reveal that no operation was cast.

## 9. Verification method

The Witness evaluates the learner's exercise output. Evidence classes:

- **OBSERVED:** the exact term strings appear (e.g., "TYPE X ✕", "INTENSITY: MEDIUM-HIGH"); each term is accompanied by a commitment sentence; intensity justifications contain pressure-words (holding, working, straining, still, pressing) vs arousal-words (sad, angry, strong feelings).
- **INFERRED:** the assignments are the best fit among the five types / five levels (the Witness may reasonably infer correct understanding from well-evidenced assignments).
- **UNKNOWN:** whether the learner *internally* performed the operation or pattern-matched the label. The Witness cannot observe cognition — only its textual residue. A perfect-looking output with portable justifications must therefore be marked as *suspected labeling*, never as confirmed understanding. Certainty about inner process is not available; say so.

Witness checks: (1) exact-term check; (2) deletion test on each invocation; (3) portability test — would this justification fit a different text? (4) arousal-word scan in intensity justifications.

## 10. Correction method

For each invoked term that fails the deletion or portability test: strip the term entirely and require the learner to rewrite the commitment *without* the vocabulary — in plain physical language tied to specific words in the text ("the repeated imperatives shorten; the body is pressing down on each one"). Only then allow the term to be re-attached, as a name for the commitment already earned. Terms that cannot survive without their symbol are not reinstated. Repeat with new micro-texts until three consecutive exercises pass all four Witness checks. Do not advance to L06 until the vocabulary is operational — L06's body test is expressed *in* these terms, and decorative vocabulary there becomes decorative reasoning.
