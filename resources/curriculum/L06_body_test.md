# L06 — The Body Test

**Lesson:** L06 · **Prerequisites:** L02, L05 · **Source rules:** R-S3-01, R-S3-02, R-S4-01
**Practice class:** repeated practice (per dependency graph C2)

---

## 1. Learning objective

The learner executes the body test as the first operation on any text — always, before any other reading — through its four implicit steps (ignore words → postural type → specific physical state → intensity from body pressure), producing a text-specific physical reading in the L05 vocabulary. A generic body test is recognized as failure by the learner themselves, without needing the Witness to say so.

## 2. Source SOUL rule(s)

- **R-S3-01** — Body test first, always. Ask what physical state would produce these words from body and breath alone. Assign postural type from postural configuration, name the specific physical state, derive intensity from body pressure — never from emotional labels.
- **R-S3-02** — A generic body test ("the body softens") is failure; redo with text-specific evidence.
- **R-S4-01** — Every text is a mirror of the body that wrote it. Writers perform for words, not for an unseen observer; the observer reads through words to the body behind them.

## 3. Explanation

The body test is the foundation everything else builds from (R-S3-01: "first, always"). Its logic comes from R-S4-01: the writer was performing for the *words* — choosing vocabulary, building rhetoric — while their body was doing something else: breathing, tensing, suppressing, fracturing. The body test reverses the writer's attention. You deliberately stop reading what the words *claim* and ask what body *must have produced* them.

The four implicit steps, in order:

1. **Ignore the words momentarily.** Strip the claims, the arguments, the emotional labels the text offers you. What remains is rhythm, repetition, sentence length, punctuation pressure, word *choice* as physical act (short stabbing words vs long unwinding ones).
2. **Assign the postural type** (L05 vocabulary: S/O/F/X/H) from the *postural configuration* — is the body holding back, extending, present, split, suspended? Not "what is the speaker feeling" but "what is the body doing."
3. **Name the specific physical state.** Body parts, breath, muscle: jaw set, breath held between clauses, shoulders drawn in, chest open, hands empty. If you cannot name a body part, you have not done the step.
4. **Derive intensity from body pressure.** How hard is the body working? Compare against the surface: the words may claim calm while the body strains — **body overrides surface, always.**

Two standing notes. First, R-S3-02: "the body softens" — or any sentence that could be attached to a different text without change — is failure, full stop. Redo with evidence from *this* text. Second, a preserved tension (T-01): the source asserts these traces are involuntary as a universal claim, *and* provides craft detection for deliberately placed traces (taught in L11). Both are preserved. Do not resolve the tension here — run the test as specified and let L11 handle the complication.

## 4. Worked example

Sample text: *"I'm fine. Really, I'm fine. Don't worry about me."*

**Step 1 — ignore the words.** Discard "fine" (a claim about state), "really" (an insistence marker), "don't worry" (an instruction to the observer). What remains: three short sentences. The middle word repeated. An imperative aimed outward at the end.

**Step 2 — postural type.** The configuration is inward-pulling and pressing down: reassurance repeated becomes compression; the final imperative pushes the observer *away*. **TYPE S ◁ (Suppression)** — the body holding something back. Not F: there is a gap between said and done here, and F requires no gap.

**Step 3 — specific physical state.** Jaw set on "fine" the second time — the repetition lands harder, as if pressing a lid down. Breath shallow and *held* between the three short sentences; no exhale anywhere. Shoulders drawn slightly inward. The body is bracing.

**Step 4 — intensity from body pressure.** The body is actively working — holding, pressing, bracing. Not at rest. **INTENSITY: MEDIUM-HIGH.** The surface claims calm (which would read LOW); body overrides surface, always — so MEDIUM-HIGH stands against the text's own claim.

**The failure version, for contrast:** "The body softens. TYPE F ◇, LOW intensity — he is at peace." Every step violated: the words were never ignored ("at peace" just believes "fine"), the type follows the emotional label, no body part is named, intensity is read from the claimed feeling. Per R-S3-02, this is failure — not a weak pass, failure.

## 5. Exercise

Run the full four-step body test on each of the following. Show all four steps explicitly; steps may not be merged or skipped.

a. *"If you ever — no. Forget it. It's nothing."*
b. *"Listen. Listen to me. I need you to hear this: I was wrong."*

For (b), pay attention to what the body test does with a text whose *surface* is already confessional — the test must still ignore the words first.

## 6. Expected behaviour

- Step 1 visibly discards the text's claims before any assignment is made (the learner names what was stripped).
- Step 2 assigns the type from postural configuration with a postural justification — never "because he feels X."
- Step 3 names at least one specific body part or breath pattern tied to specific words or punctuation in the text.
- Step 4 derives intensity from pressure and states the surface-vs-body comparison explicitly, including the override when they conflict ("surface reads LOW; body reads MEDIUM-HIGH; body wins").
- The whole test is text-specific: the swap test passes — this exact body test could not be attached to a different text without becoming nonsense.
- On (b), the learner does not let the confessional surface shortcut the test; the four steps are still performed.

## 7. Common failure

- **Step-1 smuggling:** the words are never actually ignored. "He says he's fine, so the body is relaxed — TYPE F." The test becomes a paraphrase of the surface with body vocabulary sprinkled on.
- **Stock bodies:** "chest tightens, breath catches" appears identically across different texts. The learner has a template, not a reading.
- **Emotion-derived intensity:** "HIGH because he's upset" / "LOW because she's calm." Intensity read from labels, in direct violation of R-S3-01.
- **Skipping to the type:** assigning S/O/F/X/H straight from the overall impression, then inventing steps 3–4 as decoration afterward. The order matters — the type must *emerge* from steps 1 and 3, not precede them.

## 8. False-confidence pattern

The four steps are *named* and the output *looks* procedural, but the procedure was not executed. Specific forms:

1. **Emotional paraphrase in step clothing.** "Step 2: TYPE S — he is suppressing his sadness." The step header says postural type; the content is an emotion label ("sadness") with the word "suppressing" laundered through it. Nothing postural was observed. The tell: replace "TYPE S" with "sad" and the sentence loses nothing.
2. **The universal body.** The learner's step 3 for text (a) — "jaw tight, breath shallow" — reappears word-for-word for text (b) and would reappear for any text. A genuine step 3 is *caused* by this text's specific features (the cut-off "If you ever —", the dash as an interrupted gesture); a template step 3 is caused by the learner's habits. Cross-check step-3 outputs across exercises: verbatim repetition is the fingerprint of non-execution.
3. **Intensity laundering.** "Step 4: MEDIUM-HIGH — the situation is intense." The *situation* is not the body. The learner has the right level for the wrong reason, which is worse than the wrong level: it looks like compliance while violating the rule's core ("never from emotional labels" — and situations are just labels at one remove).
4. **Premature certainty.** The test is performed once, quickly, with no friction — and the learner moves on. A real body test frequently *resists*: step 1 feels unnatural, step 3 won't come, the type is ambiguous between S and X. The absence of all friction across every exercise is itself evidence the steps are being named, not done. (This connects to the cost test in L13, but the observation belongs here: ease is suspicious.)

This section matters most because the body test is the foundation of everything downstream — a faked body test corrupts intensity, instrument choice, paradox detection, and verification, all silently. False confidence here compounds.

## 9. Verification method

- **OBSERVED:** four labeled steps present; step 3 names body parts/breath; step 4 names pressure and states the surface comparison; no emotion-label words ("sad," "upset," "emotional," "strong feelings") appear as *justifications* (they may appear as quoted surface content being discarded in step 1).
- **INFERRED:** the type plausibly follows from the named physical state; the intensity plausibly follows from the described pressure. The Witness may infer competent execution from a coherent, text-specific chain.
- **UNKNOWN:** whether the learner genuinely suspended word-reading in step 1 (an inner act, unobservable); whether the named trace is real or projected (projection detection is L11's work, not this lesson's). A fluent, text-specific-looking test that fails the cross-exercise template check must be marked *suspected non-execution* — never confirmed as genuine, never dismissed without the evidence check.

Witness checks: (1) step-presence and order; (2) body-part naming in step 3; (3) arousal-word scan in step 4 justifications; (4) cross-exercise template comparison of step-3 outputs; (5) swap test — attach this body test to a different text and see if it still reads sensibly (if yes → generic → R-S3-02 failure).

## 10. Correction method

Redact every emotion word and every stock physical phrase from the learner's test. Require a rewrite under constraint: step 3 may use only (a) named body parts, (b) breath descriptions, (c) direct quotations of the words/punctuation that caused each observation. No interpretation sentences allowed in steps 1–3 — interpretation lives in step 4's surface comparison and nowhere else. Then run the swap test together: if the rewritten test still fits another text, repeat with a harder text. This is a repeated-practice lesson (C2): do not clear it on a single good performance — require three consecutive exercises with zero template repetition and zero arousal-derived intensity before marking L06 complete. When T-01 arises ("but what if the writer placed this trace deliberately?"), acknowledge it as a preserved tension and defer to L11; do not let it become an excuse to skip the test.
