### L10-E1 output

**Truth A (obvious):** she forgave him — released him. Evidence:
"completely" unqualified, declarative finality; body — shoulders drop,
exhale (release); the sentence lands flat, finished.

**Truth B (psychological counter-reading — not factual):** she released the
*debt* but kept the *person* — the forgiveness is real and the attachment
is real. Evidence: "keeps the letter in her desk" — kept, not discarded;
a desk is intimate storage (not attic, not trash); body — a hand closing a
drawer: small, careful, repeated — she doesn't just *have* it, she *keeps*
it, and keeping implies tending. (Not "she's lying" — that would be factual
and untestable.)

**Honest testing:** Does B survive "completely"? The grammar holds both —
main clause asserts A, coordinate clause asserts B; neither is a slip.
Body evidence distinct for each: release (A) vs tending (B). Etymology:
"forgive" ← OE *forgiefan*, "to give up, give away" — the root supports A
(giving-away) while the keeping supports B. Falsifiers stated: B would be
falsified if the letter were in neutral storage or mentioned as discarded;
A would be falsified if "completely" were hedged ("sort of forgave").
**Both survive → PARADOX. Kept.**

**PARADOX field:** "Truth A: she forgave him — the grievance was given
away, completely. Truth B: she keeps him — the letter is tended in her
desk, still hers. Neither cancels the other."

**Propagation (R-S5-02):**

  to self-examining — the performed face of complete forgiveness vs the
  hidden kept attachment → Japanese Omote/Ura, not English Narrative
  Clarity. Shift stated with reason.

  forgiveness spoken in daylight, keeping done in private." Both truths

- *Meaning:* "She released him AND she keeps him. The forgiveness is real —
  the grievance was given away. The keeping is equally real — the letter is

**Uncertainty (R-S5-03):** "Cross-lens conflict named: body lens supports B
strongly (the tending gesture, drawer intimacy); depth lens (*forgiefan* =

### L10-E3 output

*"He laughed when they praised him, and he changed the subject."*

**Truth A (obvious):** he was pleased — the laugh as modest deflection,
social smoothing. Evidence: laugh + subject-change as graceful exit; body —

**Truth B (psychological counter-reading):** he distrusts the praise — the

subject-change refuses the pricing. Falsifiers: B falsified if he returned
to the praise later or acknowledged it; A falsified if the laugh read
bitter or forced (no evidence of either).

**Honest testing:** grammar holds both (laugh = social response;
subject-change = refusal); body evidence distinct for each. **Both survive
→ PARADOX. Kept.**

**PARADOX field:** "Truth A: he was pleased — the laugh was real, the
smoothing graceful. Truth B: he refused the praise — it was never
acknowledged, only evacuated. Neither cancels the other."

**Propagation:**
- *Instrument:* self-examining — the performed modesty vs the hidden
  refusal → Japanese Omote/Ura. Shift stated.
- *Tone:* "a bright laugh with a door closing behind it — pleasure
  announced, valuation declined." Both truths named.
- *Meaning:* "He laughed AND he changed the subject. The pleasure is real;
  the refusal is equally real — the praise was priced, and he declined the
  price. Neither cancels the other." No resolving conjunction.

**Uncertainty (R-S5-03):** "Cross-lens conflict named: body lens reads the
laugh as genuine pleasure (shoulders lift, exhale); elements lens
(contradiction voice: laughs / evacuates) reads the laugh as refusal.
Position A: laugh = pleasure — evidence: lift, exhale. Position B: laugh =
refusal — evidence: immediate subject-change, praise never acknowledged.
Both held; not averaged."
